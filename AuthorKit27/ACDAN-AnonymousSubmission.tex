\begin{abstract}
Test-time scaling improves language-model reasoning by sampling, searching, or
verifying multiple candidate outputs, but its cost grows quickly and its
confidence estimates are often unreliable. We study the problem of
\emph{adaptive candidate and verifier allocation}: given a task and a pool of
candidate answers or tool actions, decide which candidates deserve additional
compute, which evidence to trust, and when to stop, optimizing the
accuracy--cost--latency--calibration frontier. We present ACDAN, an
ablation-ready inference architecture that refines a soft candidate-action
distribution with Differentiable Textual Optimization (DTO), optionally uses
self-consistency evidence as an explicit controlled prior, prunes redundant
multi-step tool plans through an agentic dependency graph, reuses high-inertia
tool transitions from a separate fit split, and produces calibrated
accept/abstain decisions through self-verification. The implementation includes
neutral-order math candidate generation, an early-stopping adaptive
self-consistency baseline, real-data loaders for GSM8K, MATH-500, AIME 2025,
Omni-MATH, and BFCL, per-task artifacts, and scripts for Pareto analysis. The
paper reports the exact protocol and table templates required for the final
AAAI-27 experiments; numeric benchmark cells are intentionally left to be filled
only after the full real-backend matrix is run.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Large language models increasingly spend extra inference compute by sampling
multiple reasoning traces, reranking candidates with verifiers, or routing tasks
to stronger models. These strategies are useful but blunt: self-consistency asks
``should we sample more?'', verifier reranking asks ``which candidate is best
after scoring many candidates?'', and LLM routers ask ``which model should
answer?'' ACDAN targets the candidate-level middle ground: \emph{which candidates
deserve compute or verification, and how much?}

We position ACDAN as adaptive test-time compute allocation, not as a claim of
new intrinsic reasoning ability. The method operates over a finite action or
candidate vocabulary. In math, this is answer selection over pre-generated
candidate solutions. In tool use, this is tool/action selection over one or more
planning steps. This framing enables direct comparison with Chain-of-Thought
greedy decoding, self-consistency, early-stopping self-consistency,
Best-of-$N$ with a PRM/verifier, reward-free selectors, and model-routing
cascades.

The contribution is threefold:
\begin{itemize}
\item We formulate adaptive candidate/verifier allocation as a constrained
test-time objective over expected accuracy, token/latency cost, verifier calls,
and calibration.
\item We implement ACDAN as a reproducible inference system with DTO, explicit
self-consistency evidence controls, graph/inertia efficiency modules for
multi-step tools, and calibrated verification.
\item We provide the approval protocol for AAAI-27: neutral-order candidate
files, adaptive baselines, full math and BFCL matrices, per-task logs, and
Pareto analysis scripts. We do not fill benchmark cells until real runs complete.
\end{itemize}

\section{Problem Formulation}
For task $x$, let $\mathcal{C}(x)=\{c_1,\ldots,c_K\}$ be candidate answers,
tool calls, or action templates. A test-time policy chooses a possibly adaptive
sequence of operations $z$: sample, score with a policy prior, query a verifier
or PRM, accept, abstain, or stop. Let $a(z)$ be the selected final candidate and
$y$ the reference answer or tool trace. We optimize the frontier
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:frontier}
\max_\pi\;\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{Acc}(a(z),y)]
-\lambda_t \mathbb{E}[\mathrm{Tokens}(z)]
-\lambda_\ell \mathbb{E}[\mathrm{Latency}(z)]
-\lambda_v \mathbb{E}[\mathrm{VerifierCalls}(z)]
-\lambda_c \mathrm{ECE}(\pi),
\end{equation}
subject to a coverage constraint $\mathbb{P}[\pi\ \mathrm{answers}]\ge \rho$.
This paper evaluates methods by the empirical Pareto frontier rather than a
single scalarization.

\paragraph{Candidate diagnostics.}
For math candidate files, every run reports oracle@K, always-first accuracy,
always-last accuracy, and highest-count accuracy. These controls detect
candidate-order leakage and self-consistency plurality bias.

\paragraph{ACDAN objective.}
The core model emits candidate logits $L_0\in\mathbb{R}^{H\times V}$ for horizon
$H$ and vocabulary size $V$. DTO optimizes logits $L$ with
$P=\mathrm{softmax}(L/\tau)$:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:dto}
\begin{aligned}
J(L)=&-w_\ell \textstyle\frac{1}{H}\sum_{h,v}P_{hv}\log p_{0,hv}
-\alpha R_{\mathrm{prm}}(P)\\
&+\beta R_{\mathrm{rep}}(P)
-\gamma H_{\mathrm{dep}}(P)
-\delta_{\mathrm{sc}}S_{\mathrm{sc}}(P),
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where $S_{\mathrm{sc}}$ is an optional standardized log-count prior. In the main
math setting $\delta_{\mathrm{sc}}=0$ and candidate counts are hidden from
prompts and PRM scoring. Count evidence is only enabled in a named ablation
(\texttt{none}, \texttt{prompt}, \texttt{prm}, \texttt{dto}, \texttt{all}).

\section{Method}
\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{ACDAN Inference}
\label{alg:acdan}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\STATE Encode task $x$ and candidate/action set $\mathcal{C}(x)$; compute latent quality $q(u)$
\STATE Produce prior logits $L_0=f_\theta(x,\mathcal{C})$
\STATE Refine logits by DTO with PRM target sharpness conditioned on $q(u)$
\STATE Decode draft actions $d_h=\arg\max_v \mathrm{softmax}(L_T/\tau)_{hv}$
\STATE Overlay high-confidence inertia actions fit from train/dev traces only
\STATE Score executed actions, compute Net Information Gain, build dependency graph
\STATE Prune dead steps for cost accounting; never prune final step
\STATE Verify executed plan, calibrate confidence, then accept or abstain
\RETURN selected candidate/tool trace, confidence, cost metrics
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\paragraph{Latent-quality-conditioned PRM.}
ACDAN includes a lightweight latent reasoning block that maps the encoded task to
a state $u$ and scalar quality $q(u)\in[0,1]$. The implementation uses this
quality as a gate on verifier sharpness rather than as hidden-state adaptation of
the underlying LLM. Given raw PRM rewards $r_{hv}$, DTO receives the target
\begin{equation}
t_{hv}=\mathrm{softmax}_v\left(s(u) r_{hv}\right),\qquad
s(u)=s_0\max\{0.1,1+\eta_q(2q(u)-1)\},
\end{equation}
so higher-quality latent states produce a more decisive PRM gradient and
lower-quality states produce a flatter, more conservative target. The same latent
summary also feeds the calibration probe, but the method does not update the
base LLM's transformer weights, activations, or KV cache at inference time.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Adaptive Self-Consistency Baseline}
\label{alg:asc}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\STATE Initialize vote counts over candidate answers
\FOR{$i=1$ to $N_{\max}$}
  \STATE Add the next sample answer (or replay grouped candidate counts)
  \STATE $q \leftarrow$ current plurality count divided by samples used
  \IF{$i\ge N_{\min}$ and $q\ge \theta_{\mathrm{asc}}$}
    \STATE stop early
  \ENDIF
\ENDFOR
\RETURN plurality answer, samples used
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

Graph and inertia modules are evaluated on multi-step tools. They should not be
claimed to improve GSM8K/MATH accuracy because those are one-step
answer-selection tasks. BFCL correctness in this implementation is tool-name
sequence correctness; argument-level claims require official AST/executable
evaluation.

\section{Implementation and Reproducibility}
The repository implements:
\begin{itemize}
\item neutral-order candidate generation (\texttt{--order sample}) and legacy
candidate reordering;
\item ACDAN, CoT, SC, adaptive SC, and BoN+PRM baselines in one runner;
\item per-task artifact saving through \texttt{--save-per-task};
\item data setup for GSM8K~\citep{cobbe2021gsm8k}, MATH-500/MATH~\citep{hendrycks2021math},
AIME 2025, Omni-MATH~\citep{omnimath2024}, and BFCL~\citep{bfcl2024};
\item Pareto CSV/SVG collection through \texttt{scripts/collect\_pareto.py}.
\end{itemize}
The data setup script reads \texttt{HF\_TOKEN} from \texttt{.env} through
\texttt{load\_env()}, but the core package remains offline-friendly. All
reported final runs must store the exact result JSON, per-task rows, model IDs,
candidate file hashes, seeds, and command lines.

\section{Approval Criteria}
We will approve the method for the final AAAI-27 submission only if all of the
following are satisfied:
\begin{itemize}
\item math candidates are generated or rewritten with neutral order;
\item ASC is included as an adaptive-compute baseline;
\item main math and BFCL matrices run with per-task artifacts;
\item the Pareto plot shows ACDAN on or above the frontier versus SC, ASC, and
BoN for at least one primary dataset;
\item evidence ablations show whether gains come from prompt counts, PRM count
bonus, DTO count prior, or none;
\item BFCL argument-level claims are made only with official AST/executable
evaluation.
\end{itemize}

\section{Limitations}
Current limitations are explicit. First, math candidate selection is bounded by
oracle@K; if no candidate is correct, no selector can recover. Second, ACDAN's
current PRM/LLM-as-verifier path may still score many candidates; verifier-call
reductions must be demonstrated empirically rather than assumed. Third, graph and
inertia are efficiency modules for multi-step tool traces and are not expected to
improve one-step math accuracy. Fourth, BFCL conversion preserves gold argument
metadata, but the default runner judges only tool-name sequences; official
AST/executable evaluation is required before claiming full function-calling
correctness. Fifth, latent reasoning gates PRM sharpness through a scalar quality
score; it does not perform hidden-state test-time training of the underlying LLM.
Sixth, tau2/GAIA raw snapshots are prepared, but no final-answer
executor is integrated yet. Seventh, several fast-moving related-work citations
and dataset versions must be re-verified before camera-ready submission.

\section{Conclusion}
ACDAN reframes the project as adaptive candidate and verifier allocation for
cost-calibrated test-time inference. The repository now contains the key
submission gates: neutral candidate order controls, an adaptive
self-consistency baseline, full-matrix scripts with per-task artifacts, Pareto
analysis, and manuscript tables that require real experimental evidence before
claims are filled. The remaining scientific question is empirical: whether ACDAN
improves the accuracy--cost--calibration frontier against SC, ASC, and BoN on
the prepared math and BFCL benchmarks.

\section*{Ethical Statement}
ACDAN targets more efficient and better-calibrated test-time inference. Better
calibration and abstention can reduce confident tool-use errors, but neither is
a correctness guarantee. Candidate/verifier allocation could also make automated
agents cheaper to deploy at scale, so high-stakes use requires conservative
thresholds, domain-specific verification, and human oversight.
